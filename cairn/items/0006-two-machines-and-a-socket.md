The trunk crosses a real network. Each of two computers runs an office with a telephone on it — microphone as transmitter diaphragm, speaker as receiver, keyboard as a hand that can do only what a hand can — and the only thing that crosses the network between them is the trunk: eight thousand mu-law bytes a second, each way.

You can call a friend. The whistle still works.
