## Question

Which year, and which administration, is this office?

## Why it has to be answered first

Almost every number in the project depends on it: the tone plan, the
telephone set, the ringing voltage, the trunk signalling, the loop limits.
Mixing eras silently is the telephone equivalent of typing a colour — each
choice looks plausible and the whole describes an office that never existed.

## Options

**A Bell System step-by-step office, about 1965** (recommended). Step-by-step
was still the most common switch in service; the Western Electric 500 set
was standard; single-frequency 2600 Hz supervision was on long-haul trunks;
Notes on Distance Dialing had been published; Touch-Tone was new and rare.
Everything v0.1–v0.5 needs existed at once.

**The 1892 La Porte office.** Push-button "dials", no dial tone, a
different switch. Historically pure, and most of the interesting behaviour
(dial tone, ringback, SF trunks) did not exist yet.

**A British Strowger office (GPO).** Better-documented relay sets (A, B, C
relays are British practice), different tones and ringing. A defensible
choice that would change most constants.

## What would settle it

The owner chooses, and wherever the office deviates from that year —
because the period-correct part is undocumented, or because modelling it
buys nothing — the deviation is written down as an admission in the item
that makes it.
