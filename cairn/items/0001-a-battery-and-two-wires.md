A telephone line is a battery, a relay and a pair of copper wires, and the whole of supervision — is anybody there? — is whether current flows.

This milestone builds the foundation everything else stands on: units, the office as one netlist solved by Kirchhoff's laws on one clock, a relay that operates because of the current through its coil, and a telephone whose only way of saying anything is to close a circuit.

It ends with the first derived figure: how far from the exchange you can live before the office stops noticing you have picked up — checked against the limit the Bell System actually engineered to.

Before anything here starts, the team's own machinery has to exist: the repository, the gates, the agent definitions, and the decisions labelled `decision`, which belong to the owner.
